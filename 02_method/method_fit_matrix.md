## 2.1. Research Question (Method-Ready Precision)

To what extent can a supervised machine learning approach—comparing Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, and Random Forest—trained on financial indicators obtained from the Superintendencia de Banca, Seguros y AFP (SBS) of Peru accurately predict the credit risk classification of Peruvian financial institutions, and can the validated predictive model provide reliable decision support for financial risk assessment under a reproducible computational research framework?

# 2.4. Defense of the Winner

## Method 1 — Experimental Research (Computational Machine Learning Experiment)

### Why Method 1 wins

The research question seeks to accurately predict the credit risk classification of Peruvian financial institutions by comparing the predictive performance of multiple supervised machine learning algorithms under identical experimental conditions. This objective requires the empirical training, benchmarking, validation, and comparison of predictive models using standardized evaluation metrics.

The Computational Machine Learning Experiment is therefore the most appropriate research method because it directly evaluates predictive performance while ensuring reproducibility through controlled experimental procedures. Furthermore, this methodology is fully aligned with the Computational/Positivist research paradigm adopted in this study and reflects current methodological standards in Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning, and Financial Risk Analytics.

---

### Why not Method 2 (Design Science Research)

Design Science Research focuses on developing and evaluating technological artifacts intended to solve practical organizational problems. Although a financial decision-support system could eventually be developed using the validated predictive model, constructing such an artifact before empirically validating the predictive model would weaken the scientific contribution.

Therefore, Design Science Research is better positioned as a secondary implementation phase following model validation rather than as the primary research methodology.

---

### Why not Method 3 (Case Study Research)

Case Study Research enables an in-depth understanding of organizational processes and financial decision-making within selected institutions. However, this methodology does not allow rigorous benchmarking of multiple machine learning algorithms or objective evaluation through standardized predictive performance metrics.

Since the principal objective of this study is predictive model validation rather than organizational interpretation, Case Study Research is considered a complementary methodological alternative instead of the primary research method.

---

### One Open Tension

Although the Computational Machine Learning Experiment offers the strongest methodological alignment with the objectives of this research, financial institutions increasingly require explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) to justify automated credit-risk decisions. Consequently, balancing predictive performance with model interpretability remains an important methodological challenge, particularly in highly regulated financial environments.
