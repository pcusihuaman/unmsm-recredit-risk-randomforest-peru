# Research Question + Method-Fit Matrix
2.1 Research Question (Method-Ready Precision)

To what extent can a supervised machine learning approach—comparing Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, and Random Forest—trained on financial indicators obtained from the Superintendencia de Banca, Seguros y AFP (SBS) of Peru accurately predict the credit risk classification of Peruvian financial institutions, and can the validated predictive model provide reliable decision support for financial risk assessment under a reproducible computational research framework?

2.4 Defense of the Winner: Method 1 — Computational Machine Learning Experiment
Why Method 1 wins

The research question seeks to accurately predict the credit risk classification of Peruvian financial institutions by comparing the predictive performance of multiple supervised machine learning algorithms under identical experimental conditions. This objective requires the empirical training, benchmarking, validation and comparison of predictive models using standardized evaluation metrics. A Computational Machine Learning Experiment is therefore the most appropriate methodology because it directly measures predictive performance while ensuring reproducibility through controlled experimental procedures. Furthermore, this approach aligns with the Computational/Positivist paradigm adopted in this research and reflects current methodological standards in artificial intelligence and financial risk prediction.

Why not Method 2 (Design Science Research alone)

Design Science Research focuses primarily on the development and evaluation of technological artifacts intended to solve practical problems. Although a decision-support prototype could eventually be developed using the validated predictive model, constructing an artifact without first demonstrating the predictive validity of the underlying model would weaken the scientific contribution. Consequently, Design Science Research is more appropriately positioned as a subsequent implementation phase rather than as the principal research methodology.

Why not Method 3 (Case Study Research)

Case Study Research provides detailed contextual understanding of organizational processes and financial decision-making. However, it does not allow systematic benchmarking of multiple predictive algorithms nor objective evaluation using standardized machine learning performance metrics. Because the principal objective of this study is predictive model validation rather than organizational interpretation, Case Study Research is considered a complementary methodological alternative rather than the primary method.

One Open Tension

Although the Computational Machine Learning Experiment provides the strongest methodology for predictive accuracy, financial regulation increasingly emphasizes model transparency and explainability. Consequently, balancing predictive performance with explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) remains an important methodological challenge, particularly for financial institutions operating in highly regulated environments.
